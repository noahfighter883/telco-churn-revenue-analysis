# Executive Summary: Churn & Revenue at Risk

**Bottom line:** One customer segment — month-to-month contracts on fiber optic internet, paying by electronic check — is churning at 60% and represents $330,812/year in revenue at risk, more than 3x any other segment. A retention campaign targeting it breaks even if it saves just 8% of the customers who'd otherwise leave.

## The numbers

| | |
|---|---|
| Overall churn rate | **26.5%** |
| Revenue already lost to churn | **$139,131** (30.5% of total MRR) |
| Highest-risk segment | Month-to-month + Fiber optic + Electronic check |
| That segment's churn rate | **60.4%** (statistically significant, not noise) |
| That segment's revenue at risk | **$330,812/year** |
| Campaign breakeven point | **7.8%** effectiveness |
| Campaign ROI at 25% effectiveness | **219%** |

## The one thing to act on first

Retention spend should go to the month-to-month + fiber optic + electronic-check segment before any other — it's the top segment on both churn rate *and* dollar exposure, so there's no tradeoff to weigh. A campaign costing an estimated $25,900 only needs to retain 8 in 100 of that segment's expected churners to pay for itself.

## Two things worth investigating separately

- **Electronic check correlates with churn across every contract type**, not just month-to-month — worth treating as its own billing-friction problem, separate from contract upsells.
- **Fiber optic churns 2x higher than DSL** despite being the premium product — worth a service-quality check before assuming it's purely a pricing issue.
